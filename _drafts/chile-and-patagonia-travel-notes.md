## Day 1 (Dec 26)

Landed in Santiago after a stopover in Lima, Peru. We took an Uber to Hotel Park Plaza Bonaparte Boutique.

Ubers are very cheap in Santiago, as Uber wants to promote their services there. Unless one is planning to drive far away from Santiago, it’s best to take Uber within the city. The hotel was located in Providencia, a safe and peaceful residential area in Santiago. The neighbourhood was pretty, streets lined with flowers and tall trees, there were supermarkets, bakeries and cafes. We had dinner at Galindo ​​https://www.google.com/collections/s/list/mU2OjVth7M4KAm0Cl2s7mqHMInQhAA/u8HPQn7kPRU,

located in the vibrant Bellavista area full of restaurants. We ordered shrimp pil pil and the happy hours combo-drink (margarita and teremoto), chicken with puree picante and salmon with potato wedges, along with a jug of sangria.

On the way back, we bought the “Campari” Pisco sour-the favourite local chilean drink, fresh cherries and some doritos from a nearby mini market. Late evening we had some of the snacks and pisco to compensate for the Christmas celebration which we had missed while travelling to Chile.

## Day 2 (Dec 27)

Woke up around 8am, and feasted upon the large breakfast spread of the hotel (an assortment of breads, fresh juices, eggs, veggies, blueberry pie, bread with butter, honey and ‘dulche leche’ spread, tea cake and coffee). Then we walked along Ricardo Lyon street to downtown, soaking in the sun and old world charm of Santiago. We had lunch at Mr Fish (crab cakes, prawn fries, polenta- a stone ground corn bread, trout, salmon, merluza(fried hake) and austral beer). We went to sleep early as we had an early morning flight the next day to Puerto Montt.

## Day 3 (Dec 28)

We flew to Puerto Montt, the lake district region of central Chile. Boarding in group 1 was a privilege Latam gave to its premium economy passengers. The airline gives a nice birthday gift if you are flying on your birthday. At the PMC airport, we got a Peugeot 5008 from Salfa Rent, which we had booked prior online. It was an older version and the Carplay was not working. So we had to toggle between the phone screen and the road for maps while driving. We drove to Puerto Varas, the stopover town to Pucon, which is considered the adventure capital of Chile. This time we overlooked Pucon as we had been there last year. On the way we took a small detour to Angelmo market, the local market famous for its fresh produce as well as handcrafted wares. As I think of it now, the market gave us the most authentic flavor of the region. It is located by a small bay where painted boats were anchored. The farmers brought their wares and sold them out of trucks or straight out of the boats. We bought fresh strawberries and cherries from one such truck - they were ripe, sweet and juicy. Many shops lined the footpath laden with local wares like sheep skin of soft wool, hand-knitted sweaters and caps, toy wooly penguins and lambs, table mats made of wool. We had cream buns from a guy who was selling varieties of buns (marmalade and cream) out of a box which he was carrying on his back.

Promising to visit the market again, we drove on to Ma Patagonia, in Puerto Varas, the hostel we would stay in for the next 4 days.

Ma Patagonia hostel https://mapatagonia.cl/ was a favourite stay from last year and we were lucky to get a booking in it - we had booked 2 months in advance online.

It is always full, with guests and backpackers who are touring South America for a year or so or who are on their way to Argentina (El Calafate is a 5 hour bus journey from here). The lush green backyard garden with flowers and cherry trees is a quiet retreat for meditation or having breakfast in the warm sun. The best view from the garden is the volcano Mt. Osorno, bordering the serene Lake Llanquihue. It’s close to downtown near a lot of restaurants and bakeries. An unique hidden gem of a restaurant is Las Buenas Brasas

Recommendations from their menu: grilled hake, salmon, octopus and pedro del bosque (whisky ice cream dessert) in addition to fresh jugo naturales.

There’s a cute doll shop outside the restaurant.

We got a chocolate cake from Cafe Dane’s for Richik’s birthday and some Pisco Sour from the Santa Isabel supermercado and had a small party at the garden of Ma Patagonia, replete with flowering trees and a white, chubby cat as our merry companions.

## Day 4 (Dec 29)

We ran for 13k along lake Llanquihue on a sunny and lazy Sunday morning. On the way back we sat on the pier and dipped our tired feet in the lake. Lots of people were swimming near the lake shore. We thought of swimming the next day as we were too tired but it never arrived as there was rain and gusts of wind the next 2 days and it turned pretty cold. We ordered buffalo chicken pizza from the Good Cheese Pizzeria via UberEats and had a sumptuous dinner.

## Day 5 (Dec 30)

We drove to the Petrohue falls and the base of Mt. Osorno. There was a trail to enter the area around the Falls. The falls are very beautiful, spread around but much shorter than we expected.

Slightly underwhelmed, we walked down the trail to the Petrohue River, but towards the end it grew cloudy and started to rain.

We took refuge in the park’s cafeteria (Katarata Grill) and savoured hot coffee and a local pie.

The road to Mt. Ososrno was narrow and winding up the mountain. The foggy weather led to low visibility and we could not see even a speck of the grand volcano, so we drove back to Puerto Varas.

## Day 6 (Dec 31)

We decided to go to Chiloe, https://www.experiencechile.org/destinations/chiloe-island-western-patagonia-chile/

the famed region of the lake district of central Chile. We drove from Puerto Varas to Pargua from where we took the ferry to the Chiloe group of islands and went past Ancud to Puñihuil to see Magellanic penguins. The entire road was bordered by bunches of yellow wildflowers making us gasp in delight.

We drove through muddy unpaved narrow roads till we reached Puñihuil. The Pacific Ocean was at its gloriest, at least to us - shale green waters foaming up upon rocks and tiny islands nearby. We sat at the only restaurant (Raices restaurant) that the tiny village of Puñihuil could boast and enjoyed the sun glittering on the ocean waters and the seagulls crying afar.

We had massive XL empanadas that the restaurant boasted of and the salmon and crab empanadas were lip smacking. Today the ferry that would take us near the island habitat of the penguins, was not operating as the weather was stormy. Walking on the sand, running into the froth of the waves, picking up seaweed and shells, we had a lovely time soaking in the beauty of the Chiloe islands. After an hour of fun and frolic, we started for Castro, the capital of Chiloe - it was an hour long drive through a single kuchha road through villages and agricultural fields. The road was narrow and full of scree and undulating through very hilly structures, and we didn’t know what’d happen if a car from the opposite direction came (luckily we were all alone throughout). We waved at the farmers whom we met occasionally, busy tilling their land.

We parked our car near the UNESCO heritage Iglesia de San Francisco church and walked around the town, savouring scones and coffee. We saw the stilt houses by the Gamboa river from a viewpoint but did not cross the wooden bridge leading to them.

The sunset in summer is around 9.30pm in Chile, so when we drove back to take the ferry to Paragua, it was broad daylight though quite late in the evening. We reached our favourite restaurant Buenos Brasas in Puerto Varas only to be one of the few guests they allowed without reservation, as it was 31st December and the entire place was decked up for the year end party, teeming with people. The day’s Chiloe trip was a unique way to bid adieu to the year 2024!!

## Day 7 (Jan 1, 2025)

We took the morning flight from Puerto Montt to Punta Arenas - the gateway to the famous Torres del Paine mountains and Patagonia at its best. At the airport, we were lucky to find 4 seats in the Bus-sur which goes to Puerto Natales, as all other seats were previously booked online. Boarding a bus to Natales and there on renting a car locally is preferable. It took about 3 hours to reach Puerto Natales, passing through the vast plains of patagonian steppes, dotted with sheep, ostrich-like birds (but smaller) and ofcourse guanacos. We lugged our heavy luggages along the streets (all in grids like Coyhaique) and reached Totromundos, where we had booked an apartment online. It was the 1st of January and the entire town was closed barring a couple of restaurants and a solo mini market. We managed to get fleeced at a restaurant - Angelica where the food wasn’t great (half cooked meat) and the staff were naive (didn’t inform us about hot porcelain plates and neither did they offer any ice after our hands were burnt). The saving grace was the homemade icecream we discovered at a tiny shop. Potable water was a problem and we walked quite a bit to buy it. We also bought some provisions for our ensuing trip to the Torres del Paine national park where we were booked for the next 4 days. It’s recommended to buy provisions from Natales as food in the Park is costly with very few snacking options. We had oven baked pizzas at another restaurant - Napoli, in the evening.

## Day 8 (Jan 2)

We went for a 5k run around the neighbourhood and had a fantastic view of the bay with ships navigating through it. However we experienced the famed patagonian winds for the first time (a lot more was to come the next few days) which made it difficult for us to walk, let alone run and it was bitterly cold. After having a hearty breakfast at the hotel (the spread was very good) we were delivered a humongous Durango by “Tom Rent a Car” which our hotel Tutramundos had given the contact of. Further filling up provisions (it could almost feed an army for a couple of days) from Unimarc - the biggest departmental store in the Patagonian regions, and filling 85 litres of gasoline 97 (to last us for 4 days at the park), we were on our way to the national park. However, if your car has a smaller tank, get an extra container of gas from the petrol station itself. Do this in advance because the few petrol stations sometimes run out of gas in the park. It takes almost 2 hrs to drive from Puerto Natales to the Torres del Paine National Park. So it’s better to stay within the park premises instead of doing day trips to the park from Natales. We chose to DIY, not take tours and drive around ourselves, so if you choose that, this is the preferred way. If not, there are tours starting from Natales and also from within the park. The east side of the park is closer to the Torres del Paine trek, and other short treks to Lake Amarga, Lake Nordenskjöld, Laguna Azul and Salte Paine waterfall. So we decided to stay on the eastern side of the Park at Goiien House. The other hotels near the park entrance (Torres del Paine hotel and Ecocamps) are far too expensive. Goiien house https://www.google.com/search?q=goiien+house&oq=Goiien+house is located right from where you can see the Torres del Paine mountains as you have breakfast in the morning.

The host Viviana is warm and friendly (only be aware that this property charges to give towels). Stopping by to drop our luggage we drove on to Laguna Azul (which is outside the park premises and one can visit it without purchasing the park tickets right away). It was very windy (1st time) so take care to carry your windcheaters and a warm jacket. You can walk on the lake shore right around it. There are many tents there where you can camp. Alternatively one can take a trailer. The water was as blue as it could be. We returned to have a delicious dinner of chicken dipped in sauce with a side of mashed potatoes and vegetable soup and panna cotta. It’s a hostel so the room with bunk beds can be a bit stifling especially if your travel buddies are tall and healthy.

## Day 9 (Jan 3)

We bought park tickets and drove to the point where the Torres del Paine trek starts. We found a lot of cars parked outside the parking lot along the road- it was past 10am and we realised we were too late for the trek. Also the bitter wind drove us back to the warmth of the car. We decided to do the short trek to Salte Grande, which is a waterfall connecting lakes Pehoé and Nordenskjöld. We had to park the car on the main roadside and walk a couple of kilometers to the waterfall. But the wind speed was so high that we took refuge at the park entrance cafe, beyond which we could barely walk only 1 km fighting the wind on the way and almost falling with each step - that’s the 2nd time we faced the ill-famed patagonian winds. It’s dehydrating too so carry a good reserve of water from Natales itself (we bought our supplies from Unimarc).

Not satisfied with this failed venture to trek, we proceeded to travel to Lago Grey, as we had already driven half the distance through unpaved roads filled with moraines. There were many small and large lakes of brilliant green and blue colors along the way. There was a particular mountain whose top was shaped like an eagle spreading its wings, with its head and beak prominently standing out against the blue sky. We saw it for quite some time as we drove along from various angles as the path was winding around it.

We reached the Hotel Lago Grey at 4pm, very unsure whether we’d get the last boat ride to the glacier or not. But we were lucky to get the last catamaran at 7pm. Impromptu, we decided to stay there for a night because we were tired after our trip from Goiien house (near Laguna Amarga) to Lago Grey (east to west of torres del paine national park). Staying at hotel lago grey is the best way to board the last ferry (7pm which returns at 9.30pm) with less crowd.

The ferry ride to view the Grey glacier was very well organised. But the 40 minutes walk to the ferry from the hotel is a bit challenging due to the torrid winds that are forceful and bitterly cold. Inspite of layering up with down jackets and caps, we felt poorly clothed in front of the magnificent patagonian winds. We were welcomed with calafate sour (there were other drinks too) and given a brief of the journey and history of the place by the crew. The glacier patch in patagonia is the 3rd largest after Antarctica and Greenland. The gray color of the lake was in complete contrast to the other lakes of the region which were mostly of a green or blue hue. The view was breathtaking as we approached the glacier but one had to brave the sub zero fierce winds to step outside on the board to experience the beauty. Lots of glaciers were afloat the lake. We were informed the glacier had spread to 3 regions due to global warming. The water trapped in the ice lent a blue hue to the glacier patch. There are organisations who conduct glacier treks in the region. With many selfies and photos, we returned back to the hotel late night at 9.30pm, though the sun was yet to set.

A little about Hotel Lago Grey: unique wood cabins nestled in the woods right in front of the Grey glacier, with full view of the lago grey and the glacier. Expensive hotel but amazing breakfast (cream buns, tarts, cakes) and excellent service. The calafate sour of the hotel is a very good and refreshing drink that we had. Also the chicken sandwich was awesome alongside the combination of tomato sauce and a red herb powder.

Other activities to be done around the hotel : Go for a short a short trek from the hotel through the ferry route, from the minimarket, over a small river thru a small forest patch, over the beach onto a hill top where you can pick up calafate berries from the shrubs growing in the wild(they are very sweet) and view the Grey glacier. You can see both the Andes mountains (green with shrubs and short trees) and the Patagonian mountains(snow capped with jagged black rocks) from the hotel.

## Day 9 (Jan 4)

After enjoying a sumptuous breakfast at the hotel, we checked out and leisurely drove back from the west to the east part of the park to Goiien House. All along the path the blue or ‘azul’ colored Rio Grande river was our companion. Such splendor of nature is hard to come by.

We rested and packed in the evening and enjoyed the sun setting upon the Torres del Paine peaks which we could see from the dining hall.

## Day 10 (Jan 5)

It was time to say goodbye to the patagonian mountains. We took time out to meditate in front of the Torres del Paine sitting upon the small cement patch in the backyard of the hotel. Clouds were gathering in the sky and soon the mountain peaks were submerged in them. As if nature’s heart too was clouded like ours for parting ways.

We drove back to Puerto Natales but took a detour on the way to visit caves dug by huge prehistoric animals called Molodons. The stone caves were huge and had stalactites hanging from the roofs. A lot of research work has been done to determine the age of the rocks and the animals associated with each age. The largest cave was closest to the park entrance. The other 2 smaller caves were about a half-hour walk away through forest paths. We returned to have barbecue chicken pizzas at Mesita Grande. For the night we checked in at Arte Brisa boutique hostel, from where we could see the bay and the horizon. We had a very nice dinner at Mar de 4 Plaza downtown.

## Day 11 (Jan 6)

Today we drove around the town searching for warm leggings but couldn’t find any to our liking. Depositing the Durango at the bus station, we boarded the Bus-Sur to Punta Arenas. After 3 hours by Bus-sur from Puerto Natales, it took an hour for Ecorent to give us the Subaru Forester car at the Punta Arenas airport. We drove to our lodging for the day - Departmento Manantiales - a nice townhome within a compound that had automatic gates. It was on the Manantiales avenue, which was a residential area with nice restaurants around. Also the road led down to the Magellan de Estrocho, which is the Magellan strait facing promenade where one can walk, jog or cycle for kilometers.The beach is accessible from portions of the promenade, where we found molluscs stuck on rocks and a lot of seaweed. The sand was black in color. We had lunch at El Arriero as suggested by our hostess. Very good food of lamb risotto and salmon with baked veggies. We went for an evening run along the Magellan strait, visited the status of Magellan and Ona (the local Indian, whose toe if one kisses is sure to return back to Punta Arenas).

## Day 12 (Jan 7)

Today we came to Porvenir from Punta Arenas by the Tabsa ferry thru the Magellan strait. We saw dancing dolphins. Staying at Cabanas Pulegan. Had lunch at Hotel Puerto Montt, the chicken milanese and raspberry juice was brilliant. Really recommend it. We saw pristine lakes like the Serano where we soaked our feet but promised to return for a swim which didn’t happen), lago de los Cisnes(3 lakes), Laguna Verde, Monumental and the white salt lake. We saw many animals including the guanco, fox, black necked swans, albatross, lambs. Had ‘helados’ and waffles at Munas Australis. Had dinner at the hotel’s restaurant. Puree picante and salmon and chicken soup was good.

## Day 13 (Jan 8)

Big day - today was the visit to the King Penguin park - the Penguino Rey Park run by a private entity who looks after the preservation of the king penguin colony at about 135 km south of Porvenir. We drove by the Magellan Strait seeing pristine beaches and guanaco filled vast shrub land. The road was a gravel unpaved one, not too bad - a major portion was under construction so for future tourists it's good news. We had fairly good weather though it was cloudy and windy but thankfully no rain. Atreyo drove the last couple of kms @100 kmph and made it to the park by 12 o’clock. The tour had started but we managed to catch up with the tour guide Christopher near the penguin colony. It was a surreal experience -difficult to believe that I was actually seeing a penguin - that I had come so far to the end of the world to see a penguin. The birds were very pretty - white stomached, orange and yellow necked. It was incubation time and majority of the king penguins were standing together with eggs on the feet covered by their stomach feathers for 2 mths. The single penguins stood far away hobbling around, sleeping with their necks turned 180 deg behind, so flapping their wings, some swimming. We took pictures from the binoculars as the birds were 30ft away. On the way we were educated further about Chilean tuku-tuku, a mouse that digs very deep caves and keeps the oxygen flowing in the soil, some hen like birds.also about prehistoric flora of green patch of grass with mini yellow flowers. We bought a couple of penguin T-shirts for the boys and started on our way to the Cameron fishing village but torrential rains and high speed winds forced us to turn back towards Porvenir. On the way we stopped at pristine beaches beside the straight. The wind nearly blew us away. It was 11 deg but felt like zero deg with winds @ 44kmph.

Buying some grocery from the mini markets and some helados from Bocados - we had a decent dinner at home with hake and chicken milanese.

The road was almost concrete with very little unpaved portion. Lot of road construction was going on – hopefully the next time the travel should be better. It’s the way to Natales maybe that’s why.

## Day 15 (Jan 10)

Woke up at 6am to take the 8am ferry from Porvenir to Punta Arenas. It was super windy and cold so despite being out on the deck for an hour we could not see any dolphin. We had bfast of eggs cooked by Ma and baba in the morning and bread with butter/ Dulche leche (date flavored) and had coffee from the cafe in the ferry. We saw a documentary of spaceX on the tv of the ferry, and reached Punta arenas by 11am. We parked our car in the main plaza de armas and bought some nice souvenirs (fridge stickers, shirts, small cups and sweaters) from the roadside stalls and from an old antique/gift shop. We visited the statue of Magellan and his trusted aide Ona (and per tradition Ma kissed his toes). We also spent some time in Santander work cafe, then we went to lunch at Mesita grande and had bbq chicken/shrimp pizza, lamb chops, lamb pasta with fettuccini, garlic sticks and avocado salad with Calafate sour and Ona cream. We then had a leisurely stroll along the coastline (they had a nice walkway along the ocean with large monuments, sundials, sculptures). We then went to our hotel La casa Escondida which was a cabin hidden in the woods in a forest by the road to the airport up an unpaved road, and the owner was Mauricio Ovando, an old man with prying eyes. After a brief afternoon nap, Richu washed the car with a hose (after Mauricio wet me fully) and all of us drove to the promenade and did a short 5k run/walk and learnt that the small birds looking like penguins were actually imperial cormorants. Then we had dinner at La Arrierto where we had lamb appetizer, calafate sour (and austral beer), nice fried pan with salsa, then lamb risotto and salmon with grilled veggies and delectable gravy. We had a wonderful time listening to a jolly old folk singer from Punta arenas who was with guitar in hand and red wine on the table. Came back to the cabin where the old owner was awaiting, and had a quick sleep after a bath.

Punta Arenas

We took the 8am ferry back to Punta Arenas. The other ferry was at 8.30pm (it being a Friday). The TABSA ferry time varies daywise – on Monday it doesn’t operate. Tuesdays are 8.30am and 5.30pm from Punta arenas. Return from Porvenir varies with 2pm on thrusdays and 8.30pm on Fridays. Since we had an early morning flight the next day to Santiago from Punta arenas, we had to take the 8am ferry back from Porvenir – wasting an entire day and leaving this beautiful island – we had fallen in love with Tierra del Fuego.

The hotel (La Casa Escondida) refused to give us an early check-in so we drove to the Plaza de Armas after deboarding from the ferry. We found an empty parking spot by the Ona park and shopped to our hearts content from the local handicraft fellow. We bought hand-knitted sweaters, dolls and locally crafted pen stands apart from fridge stickers. The buildings were old and well designed around the place. The Estrecha de Maganelles was a nice sea promenade offering unending view of the Maganelles strait and congruence of the cormorants on the piers. We ran along the stretch in the evening. We had lunch at Mesita Grande – the lamb was hard to chew but we had Ona-cream apart from our favorite Calafate sour. In the evening post our run, we revisited the popular joint El Arirreo and reordered their lamb risotto. It was a fitting dinner to welcome Atreyo’s birthday.

## Day 16 (Jan11)

It was our day to bid adieu to the magical Patagonia and Tierra del Feugo and fly back to Santiago. We flew over frozen lakes and ice capped mountains in the southern archipelago towards drier mountains as we headed towards northern Chile.

Santiago always adds a dash of spice to our travels with its vibrant city life - we city mongrels get back our mojo. Also the Providencia locality of Hotel Bonaparte is very nice, I feel it is seeped in European culture, Richu feels like Bandra. We literally hogged at Don Peyo but this time it was more crowded. The same waiter served us last year. The sangria jugo, mas pan, salsa, breaded chicken, salmon frio was prepared to the T. The tres dulce dessert was a perfect way to celebrate Atreyo’s 27th birthday. We fished out a choco torte cake from the nearby Pasteleria and celebrated in the garden attached to our room.
